# Farm-Production-Profitability-Performance-Analysis

Agriculture plays an important role in food production, employment, and economic development. However, understanding the relationship between production output, operating costs, revenue, and profitability is essential for making informed agricultural decisions.

This project uses Microsoft Power BI to analyze farm production and profitability across different crops, states, and regions. It explores seasonal yield, farmers' years of experience, transportation costs, and the availability of essential farm facilities.

The goal is to transform agricultural data into meaningful insights that can help farmers, agricultural businesses, and decision-makers improve resource allocation, evaluate financial performance, and identify areas requiring further investment.

# Objectives

## The analysis was designed to:

Evaluate overall farm production, revenue, costs, and profitability.

Compare the financial performance of different crops.

Identify states and regions contributing significantly to total profit.

Examine production yield across seasons and irrigation conditions.

Explore the distribution of farmers by years of experience.

Investigate transportation costs across different distance categories.

Assess the availability of farm facilities and storage infrastructure.

Develop practical, data-driven recommendations for improving agricultural performance.

# Tools and Technologies

Microsoft Excel: Data organization and preparation.

Microsoft Power BI: Dashboard development, visualization, and analysis.

DAX (Data Analysis Expressions): Measures and calculations, where applicable, for evaluating performance indicators.

# Key Findings

Based on the displayed dashboard:

Positive reported financial performance: Total revenue of ₦45.86 billion exceeds total cost of ₦11.9 billion, with reported total profit of ₦33.97 billion.

Differences in state profitability: Ogun and Benue are among the leading states in the displayed profit ranking.

Regional performance differences: North Central leads the displayed regional profit and production comparisons.

Potential infrastructure gaps: Approximately 3,000 farm records are reported as having no facilities, indicating an area requiring further investigation.

Seasonal production patterns: Comparing yield across seasons and irrigation conditions can help identify opportunities for more effective production planning.

Variation in crop-level financial performance: Revenue, cost, and profit differ across crops, supporting the need for crop-specific financial evaluation.

Transportation expenditure differences: Total costs vary across distance categories, but additional measures are needed to compare transportation efficiency fairly.

# Recommendations

1. Improve Farm Infrastructure

Investigate why many farms lack essential facilities and determine which investments would provide the greatest benefit. Storage, irrigation, and other facilities should be prioritized according to local production needs, cost, and expected returns.

2. Make Crop Selection More Data-Driven

Compare crop profit margins, profit per hectare, production costs, market demand, and price stability before allocating more resources to particular crops.

3. Investigate Regional Performance

Study the factors associated with stronger regional production and profitability. Consider crop distribution, input prices, farm size, market access, and infrastructure before applying lessons to other regions.

4. Evaluate Transportation Efficiency

Calculate average transportation cost per farm, per shipment, or per tonne. These measures would provide a fairer comparison than total category costs and help identify realistic opportunities to reduce logistics expenditure.

5. Optimize Seasonal Production

Compare seasonal yield and production costs under different irrigation conditions. Evaluate whether irrigation investments are economically beneficial for particular crops and locations.

6. Strengthen Farmer Training and Knowledge-Sharing

Use farmers' experience categories to plan appropriate training and mentorship programmes. Compare production and profitability across experience groups to identify specific areas where additional support may be useful.

7. Improve Financial Monitoring

Track revenue, total cost, profit, profit margin, and profit per hectare. These indicators can help stakeholders distinguish between high production volume and genuinely strong financial performance.

#  Potential Additional Metrics

The existing dashboard can be extended with the following measures to deepen the analysis.

Profit margin

Profit margin measures the proportion of revenue retained as profit.

Profit Margin (%) = (Total Profit / Total Revenue) × 100

Using the displayed figures:

(₦33.97bn / ₦45.86bn) × 100 ≈ 74.0%

This is an indicative calculation based on the reported totals. The result should be verified against the underlying dataset and the definition of profit used in the analysis.

Average transportation cost per tonne

Average Transport Cost per Tonne = Total Transportation Cost / Total Tonnes Transported

This would help compare transportation efficiency across distance categories, provided both measures cover the same records and period.

Profit per hectare

Profit per Hectare = Total Profit / Total Cultivated Hectares

This would help compare financial productivity across crops, states, and regions where cultivated area is available and measured consistently.
